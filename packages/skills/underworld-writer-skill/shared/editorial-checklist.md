# Editorial Checklist: Pre-Submission Audit

Run this checklist **before** your manuscript leaves your hands.

---

## Dialogue Audit

- [ ] **Invented dialogue:** Scan for dialogue NOT in quotation marks or attributed to a source. Examples:
  - ✗ "Johnson must have realized they were closing in" → Is this from an interview/statement?
  - ✗ "She nervously picked up the phone" → Is this from a witness account?
  - ✓ "According to the FBI affidavit, Johnson said…" → Cited ✓

- [ ] **Paraphrased dialogue verification:** For each paraphrased statement (not in quotes), have I cited the source in the same paragraph or a footnote?
  - ✗ "Johnson later explained his motivation" (no citation)
  - ✓ "Johnson later explained his motivation (Darknet Diaries, Ep. 128)" ✓

- [ ] **Courtroom dialogue:** For trial scenes, have I verified each piece of dialogue against the trial transcript?
  - If transcript is not available, have I marked it as paraphrased from news reporting (Tier 3)?

---

## Unexplained Specificity Audit

Scan for specific details that feel "too real" but have no source. Examples:

- "Johnson was 22 years old when he founded ShadowCrew" ← Do you have his DOB from PACER?
- "The data breach occurred at 3:47 AM on March 15, 2003" ← Is this from the FBI affidavit?
- "He lived in a modest apartment in northeast Pennsylvania" ← Is this from court documents or journalist reporting?

**For every specific date, time, age, location, name, or number:**
- [ ] Have I verified it against a Tier 1–3 source?
- [ ] Have I cited it clearly (in-text or footnote)?

---

## Unsourced Dates Audit

Go through your manuscript and highlight every date. For each one:

- [ ] Is the date from a court record (Tier 1), interview (Tier 2), or published reporting (Tier 3)?
- [ ] If it's from reporting, have I checked against PACER for accuracy?
- [ ] If the date differs across sources, have I noted the discrepancy?

Example:
```
Unsourced: "Johnson's first hack occurred in 1998."
Sourced: "According to Johnson's interview with Darknet Diaries, his first 
successful breach was in 1998, when he was still a teenager. (Darknet Diaries Ep. 128)"
```

---

## Single-Source Items Audit

Identify claims that come from only ONE source. These are vulnerable.

For each single-source item:
- [ ] Is it a fact (easily verifiable) or an interpretation (open to dispute)?
- [ ] Can I find corroboration in a second source?
- [ ] If not, should I note it as disputed or mark it with `[GAP — searched, no corroboration]`?

Example:
```
Single source: Johnson's motive comes only from Darknet Diaries interview.
Question: Is this sufficient, or should I note this as his account (Tier 2) without independent corroboration?
Decision: Note it as Johnson's account; he's the primary source on his own motivation.
```

---

## Character Interiority Audit

Search your manuscript for phrases like:
- "Johnson felt"
- "He thought"
- "She realized"
- "He wanted"
- "They believed"

For each one:
- [ ] Does this come from an interview or statement (Tier 2)?
- [ ] Or is this my inference from his actions?

**If it's inference:**
- Mark it with "Johnson appeared to…" or "The investigators suspected…" (hedging)
- Or remove it entirely

Example:
```
Unsourced: "Johnson felt empowered by each successful breach, feeding his addiction to hacking."
Sourced: "Johnson described his early hacking as driven by curiosity. In Darknet Diaries, he 
reflected: 'I wanted to see if I could do it.' (Ep. 128)"
Better than: "Johnson felt empowered by each successful breach" — stick to his words.
```

---

## Narrative Reconstruction Audit

For scenes reconstructed from logs, exhibits, or forensic evidence:

- [ ] Have I clearly marked this as reconstruction (author's note, in-text attribution)?
- [ ] Have I avoided inventing dialogue or emotion in the reconstruction?
- [ ] Have I sourced the underlying evidence (FBI affidavit, server logs, transaction records)?

Example (Good Reconstruction):
```
Based on server logs recovered during the takedown, investigators determined that 
Johnson accessed the database four times between March 1 and March 8, 2003. Each 
session lasted approximately 45 minutes. (FBI Affidavit, dated March 20, 2003, PACER)
```

Example (Poor Reconstruction):
```
Johnson hacked into the server late at night, his fingers flying over the keyboard 
as he searched for credit card data. He was careful, methodical, scanning for security 
measures as he went deeper into the system.
```
← No source! Reads like invention.

---

## Quotes & Paraphrase Balance Audit

- [ ] Is every sentence either a direct quote (with quotation marks) or clearly attributed paraphrase?
- [ ] Have I avoided using quotation marks for non-verbatim statements?
- [ ] Have I avoided running quotes without introducing them?

Example (Poor):
```
Johnson said "I participated in the conspiracy and sold stolen credit cards for profit." 
This was a serious admission. "I knew it was illegal" but he did it anyway.
```

Example (Good):
```
At his plea hearing, Johnson told the judge: "I knowingly and intentionally participated 
in the conspiracy and sold stolen credit cards for profit." (PACER, case 03-CR-0322) 
He acknowledged the illegality of his actions and accepted responsibility.
```

---

## Gap Notation Audit

- [ ] Have I used `[GAP]` or `[GAP — searched, not public]` consistently?
- [ ] Have I avoided using `[GAP]` for things I simply haven't searched for yet?

Correct uses:
- `[GAP — searched: PACER, NewsBank, FBI archives; not found]`
- `[GAP — Johnson's employment history pre-ShadowCrew not publicly available]`

Don't use:
- `[GAP]` without explanation
- `[GAP]` for things you haven't yet tried searching for

---

## Author's Note Verification

- [ ] Have I written an author's note explaining my sourcing methodology?
- [ ] Does it cover:
  - [ ] Which sources I considered Tier 1, 2, and 3?
  - [ ] Which claims I could not source (gaps)?
  - [ ] Any reconstructed scenes and their basis?
  - [ ] Any composite characters (none should exist in true crime)?
  - [ ] Use of pseudonyms or initials for victim/minor privacy protection (if used, disclose in author's note)?
  - [ ] Handling of victim privacy and minor characters?

---

## Final Read-Through

- [ ] **Specificity test:** Is every specific claim (date, name, number, location) clearly sourced?
- [ ] **Dialogue test:** Is every piece of dialogue from a documented source or clearly marked as paraphrase?
- [ ] **Emotion test:** Have I avoided inventing feelings or thoughts without source?
- [ ] **Fact-check test:** Can someone reading my book point to a source for every major claim?

---

## Submission Checklist

Before you submit the manuscript:

- [ ] All sources are documented (PACER, DOJ press, journalist reporting, interview)
- [ ] All dialogue is quoted or paraphrased with attribution
- [ ] No invented interiority or speculation without hedging
- [ ] Author's note explains sourcing and methodology
- [ ] Gaps are explicitly marked with escalation explanation
- [ ] Victim privacy is respected
- [ ] The manuscript is factually verifiable

**If any box is unchecked, do not submit. Revise first.**
