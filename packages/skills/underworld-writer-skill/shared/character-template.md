# Character Template: Real People in True Crime Narrative

## Citation Rule for Real Characters

**Classify each real person by public status before deciding sourcing rules:**

- **Public figures** (defendants, prosecutors, investigators named in media coverage): May be profiled more extensively; sourcing standards remain the same (Tier 1-3)
- **Private individuals** (victims, minor witnesses, unnamed relatives): Limit details to what appears in court records; do not profile or elaborate beyond court filings unless they consent to interview
- **Law enforcement/officials** (named agents, judges, prosecutors): Treat as public figures; cite official titles from court records

**For all real people, never invent:
- Dialogue (unless sourced from interview/court record)
- Thoughts or feelings
- Motivations (unless the person states them on record)
- Physical descriptions (unless on wanted poster, driver's license, or in court document)
- Personality traits (use only what sources demonstrate through action)

```

**Tier 2 Source:** [Darknet Diaries interview / FBI oral history / documentary]
**Tier 3 Source:** [Journalism profile / news reporting]

**Role in Narrative:** [Their function in the crime: perpetrator, investigator, victim, informant]

**Verifiable Details:**
- Age/DOB: [from indictment]
- Location: [from court records]
- Online handle: [from government exhibits]
- Known associates: [from indictment co-conspirators list]
- Crime(s): [from charging documents]
- Sentence: [from sentencing order]
- Outcome: [from post-sentencing records]

**Dramatization Limits:**
- Can we show internal thoughts? NO (unless from interview)
- Can we show dialogue? ONLY if from Tier 1–2 source
- Can we show reaction? YES if documented (arrest photo, trial testimony)
- Can we extrapolate motive? NO, unless person states it

**Sample Scene (if permitted):**
[If character is allowed to speak, example from which court/interview source]

**Red Flags to Avoid:**
- Do not describe their childhood unless they state it on record
- Do not infer their reasons for crime; quote them if available
- Do not imagine their feelings during arrest; use body language from police report if documented
- Do not give them qualities they haven't demonstrated in sources
```

```

**Tier 1 Source:** United States v. Johnson, 03-CR-0322, Middle Dist. FL (PACER)
**Tier 2 Source:** Darknet Diaries Ep. 128–129 (Brett Johnson interview)
**Tier 3 Source:** Wired, KrebsOnSecurity, FBI press release (October 2004)

**Role in Narrative:** Perpetrator; ShadowCrew founder and leader; later, FBI informant

**Verifiable Details:**
- **Born:** [Verify from PACER docket before use; this template does not include actual DOB]
- Location: Pennsylvania (indictment shows IP logs)
- Handle: Primary alias used in indictment and media
- Known associates: [From indictment co-conspirator list]
- Primary crime: [From charging documents]
- Sentence: [From sentencing order]
- Outcome: [From post-conviction records]

**NOTE:** This example is a *template*. Do not use specific details (associates, handles, sentences) without verifying them against actual PACER dockets, court records, or authenticated interviews. Using unverified details contradicts the skill's core principles.

**Dramatization Limits:**
- Can we show internal thoughts? NO (Darknet interview only gives retrospective reflection)
- Can we show dialogue? YES, verbatim from Darknet Diaries transcript; can paraphrase with attribution
- Can we show reaction? YES when sourced (e.g., "According to the FBI affidavit, agents observed…")
- Can we extrapolate motive? YES, because Brett states in Darknet Diaries interview why he started ShadowCrew

**Sample Scene (PERMITTED):**
Brett describes his approach to the server breach (from Darknet Diaries Ep. 128, ~17:30):
"I went in through what I thought was going to be a backdoor. When I first hit it, I was like, this is not going to work. This is too protected."

*Dramatized version with attribution:*
"When Brett first approached the server, his reconnaissance suggested a backdoor was possible. But when he tried it, he realized the defenses were tighter than expected. 'This is not going to work,' he thought. 'This is too protected.' (Darknet Diaries, Ep. 128)"

**Red Flags to Avoid:**
- Don't describe Brett's childhood yearnings; he doesn't discuss them in Darknet Diaries
- Don't infer his reasons for joining the carder underground; use his Darknet Diaries quote about wanting to test himself
- Don't imagine his fear during arrest; no account of that exists; stick to what the affidavit says about his behavior
- Don't call him a "visionary" or "brilliant strategist" unless the journalist or his own interview establishes those qualities
```

## Dialogue Sourcing Rules

### Tier 1 Dialogue (Court Records)
- **Source:** Trial transcript, plea agreement reading, sworn affidavit
- **Format:** Verbatim from PACER
- **Attribution:** Footnote to case number and page

Example:
```
At his plea hearing, Johnson told the judge: "I knowingly and intentionally 
participated in the ShadowCrew conspiracy." (United States v. Johnson, 
03-CR-0322, Plea Hearing Transcript, Aug. 15, 2004, PACER)
```

### Tier 2 Dialogue (Direct Interview)
- **Source:** Documented interview (Darknet Diaries, documentary, FBI recorded interview)
- **Format:** Verbatim from transcript, OR clearly marked paraphrase
- **Attribution:** In-text citation

Example (Verbatim):
```
In his Darknet Diaries interview, Johnson recalled: "I wanted to see if I could do it. 
That's basically where it all started." (Darknet Diaries Ep. 128)
```

Example (Paraphrase):
```
Johnson explained to Dan Cody that his initial motivation was simply curiosity—he wanted 
to test his own abilities. (Darknet Diaries Ep. 128)
```

### Tier 3 Dialogue (Journalistic Paraphrase)
- **Source:** News article quoting interview or court testimony
- **Format:** Paraphrased, attributed to journalist's reporting
- **Note:** Treat as less reliable than Tier 1–2

Example:
```
According to a Wired profile, Johnson described his hacking as "just exploring 
what the systems were capable of." (Wired, [date])
```

### NO: Invented Dialogue
❌ "Johnson sneered at the federal agents: 'You'll never catch all of us.'"
❌ "He told his girlfriend that night, 'This is bigger than I thought.'"
❌ "In his mind, Brett repeated: *Why am I doing this? For the money? For power?*"

**All dialogue must come from a documented source.**

## Physical Appearance & Description Rules

### Permitted (from official sources):
- ✓ Height/weight from wanted poster or booking photo
- ✓ Tattoos documented in court exhibits
- ✓ Scars mentioned in police report
- ✓ Clothing worn during arrest (from police report or trial testimony)

### Not permitted (unsourced speculation):
- ✗ "Johnson was lean and intense, with sharp features"
- ✗ "His eyes betrayed a criminal intellect"
- ✗ "Brett looked older than his years"

**If you need to describe a character's appearance, cite the source.**

Example:
```
The booking photo from his 2003 arrest showed Johnson as a lean, sharp-featured man 
in his early twenties, with dark hair and an unsettling gaze. (Middle Dist. FL, PACER, 
case 03-CR-0322, Exhibit A)
```

## Motivations: Show, Don't Guess

Don't write: "Johnson craved the thrill of crime and the prestige of the hacker world."

Do write: "In interviews, Johnson described his early hacking as driven by curiosity. 'I wanted to see if I could do it. That's basically where it all started.' Later, as ShadowCrew grew, the financial incentives became apparent—he was making thousands of dollars from stolen card sales. (Darknet Diaries Ep. 128)"

Use the person's own words when available. When not available, note the gap.

---

## Real vs. Composite Characters

**This skill does NOT support composite characters for factual narratives.**

If you're combining multiple people into one character:
- ✗ Not permitted in true crime
- **Correct approach:** Write about each real person separately, with their own sources

If someone is known by multiple names or aliases:
- ✓ Permitted; one real person with multiple identities (e.g., "Porkchop" = Brett Johnson)
- Use the alias the person was known by during the crime, then clarify identity once

## Minor Characters & Victims

For crime victims and minor witnesses who aren't public figures:

- **Use only published names** (from court records, press releases, or journalist reporting)
- **Avoid invented details** about their lives before the crime
- **Do not dramatize their experience** beyond what they've stated in interviews/testimony
- **Consider privacy:** Even if a victim's name is public, excessive detail about them may violate victim privacy norms

Example (Acceptable):
"The breach affected millions of cardholders. One victim, identified in court documents as T.M., discovered fraudulent charges on his account in December 2003."

Example (Not Acceptable):
"Maria, a single mother working two jobs, had just paid down her mortgage when her identity was stolen. The emotional toll was immense, forcing her to move in with her parents."
← Don't invent victim's backstory; don't create emotional narrative without their permission

## Character Checklist Before Submission

For every real person in your narrative:

- [ ] Have I cited at least one Tier 1 or Tier 2 source for each person?
- [ ] Are all quoted statements tied to a documented source?
- [ ] Have I avoided inventing dialogue, thoughts, or motivations?
- [ ] Have I described only what sources actually document?
- [ ] Have I attributed descriptions and actions clearly?
- [ ] Have I honored victim privacy where appropriate?
- [ ] Have I distinguished between what the person says and what I infer?

**If any box is unchecked, go back and add sources or remove the detail.**
